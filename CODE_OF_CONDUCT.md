# Community Code of Conduct

## Our standard

Legion Control welcomes constructive participation from people of all
backgrounds and experience levels. We want technical disagreement to produce
safer software, not hostility.

Expected behavior includes:

- treating people with respect and patience;
- criticizing ideas and evidence rather than people;
- stating uncertainty and correcting mistakes openly;
- respecting privacy, especially in hardware logs and security reports;
- prioritizing user safety over speed, status, or feature pressure;
- accepting maintainer decisions about unverified hardware writes without
  harassing contributors or asking them to take unsafe risks.

Unacceptable behavior includes:

- harassment, threats, discrimination, sexualized attention, or personal
  attacks;
- trolling, repeated disruption, or deliberately inflammatory comments;
- publishing another person's private information or device identifiers;
- pressuring someone to disclose a vulnerability publicly;
- encouraging dangerous thermal, power, firmware, or privilege experiments;
- impersonation or knowingly misleading compatibility claims.

## Scope

This standard applies in repository issues, pull requests, reviews,
discussions, and other spaces where someone represents the project. It also
applies to public conduct that directly targets project participants or
materially affects their ability to participate safely.

## Reporting conduct problems

Use GitHub's built-in content-reporting or abuse-reporting tools for harassment,
threats, doxxing, or platform-policy violations. A public issue may be used for
a non-sensitive moderation request, but do not disclose private personal or
security information there.

The project does not currently publish a dedicated confidential conduct-report
address and therefore cannot promise confidentiality through a project-owned
channel. If such a channel is added, this document will be updated before it is
used.

Security vulnerabilities follow [`SECURITY.md`](SECURITY.md), not this conduct
process.

## Enforcement

Maintainers may edit or remove contributions, close or lock discussions, issue
a warning, temporarily restrict participation, or permanently ban a participant
when behavior threatens safety or continued constructive work. Decisions will
consider context, severity, repetition, impact, and whether the participant
takes responsibility and changes their behavior.

Retaliation against someone who raises a good-faith concern is itself a
violation. Knowingly false or manipulative reports may also be acted upon.
